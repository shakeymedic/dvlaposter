Fitness to drive

Does your patient have any of these? Check the DVLA guidance.

Seizures or blackouts

Any seizure, unexplained loss of consciousness

Brain

Stroke, TIA, head injury, intracranial bleed, tumour, disabling vertigo

Heart

ACS, arrhythmia, pacemaker or ICD, aortic dissection or aneurysm

Diabetes

Severe hypo, loss of hypo awareness, new insulin

Mental health

Psychosis, mania, severe depression, suicidal thoughts, dementia

Alcohol or drugs

Dependence or persistent misuse

Eyes

Poor vision, double vision, visual field loss

Sleepiness

Sleep apnoea, excessive daytime sleepiness

It says whether they must stop driving, for how long, and whether to tell DVLA.

AdviseExplain the patient's legal duty to tell DVLA.
SignpostPoint them to GOV.UK. DVLA decides when they can drive.
DocumentRecord what driving advice you gave.

Group 1

Cars, motorcycles, and vans up to 3.5 tonnes (most delivery drivers).

Group 2 stricter rules

Lorries over 3.5 tonnes (C1, C), minibuses and buses (D1, D).

Drives for work? Ask what they drive.

  • Taxi and private hire: councils should apply Group 2 rules.
  • Delivery: van up to 3.5 t is Group 1; larger lorry is Group 2.
  • Paramedics, police, fire: rules for the vehicle they drive (ambulance over 3.5 t is Group 2). Employers may set higher standards.
Won't stop driving? Speak to a senior about telling DVLA without consent.
